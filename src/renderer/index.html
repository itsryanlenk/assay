How it works.

Assay finds one local business, checks its own page source, and drafts an outreach packet. It never sends anything. The whole design exists to make one promise true: every claim in a deliverable can be reproduced by the recipient with Ctrl+U. Reach this page any time from Help in the menu bar or the nav above.

THE FLOW

Five steps, in order.

  • 1 · Find. On Scan, either enter a city and a category and pull candidates from the Google Places API, or switch to Scan a URL and type the address of a business you already know, which needs no key. No Maps HTML is scraped either way. A typed address carries no listing at all, so the NAP check records that it had nothing to compare and can never report a mismatch, and the schema kit says the facts came from you rather than from a listing.
  • 2 · Check. Press Check on a candidate to run the six flaw checks against its own fetched page source. Findings come back REMOTE: real, logged, and not yet safe to send.
  • 3 · Confirm. Press Confirm, open each named document, press Ctrl+U, and paste the source. The same checks re-run against your bytes, and a finding becomes CONFIRMED or DIVERGED.
  • 4 · Generate. Once the packet is releasable, Generate the packet writes the scorecard, schema kit and drafts and records every one as PREPARED.
  • 5 · Approve. In Approvals, clear each artifact one at a time. Approval is per item and re-checks the confirmation. There is no unapprove; reject with a reason instead.
THE SIX CHECKS

What each one looks at.

  • Website. Does the listed site load, and is it really theirs.
  • Crawl and index. robots.txt, canonical tags, and whether a sitemap resolves to real pages.
  • AI readiness. The 105-point instrument: crawler access, llms.txt, entity schema, FAQPage, product and offer schema, and plain-words vocabulary.
  • Freshness. How recently the content and its machine-readable dates were updated.
  • Booking path. Whether a customer can actually book or reach the business from the page.
  • NAP consistency. Name, address and phone agreeing across the site and the listing.

Ranking takes the single worst finding, never an average: outreach leads with one problem too good to sit on.

CONFIRMATION

Why REMOTE is not enough.

What this app's crawler receives is routinely not what you see in Ctrl+U: bot challenges, consent walls, CDN and geo variance. Because the pitch rests on the prospect reproducing every claim, a claim only the crawler saw is worse than no claim at all.

  • REMOTE. Crawler-only. Real and logged, but nothing can be generated or sent from it.
  • CONFIRMED. Your own view-source reproduces the finding. Safe to use.
  • DIVERGED. Your source contradicts the crawl. The original claim is void, and a site that answers crawlers differently than browsers is itself worth raising.

Confirmations expire 72 hours after they are made and do not survive a restart, on purpose: a stale confirmation must never look live.

THE VIEWS

What each screen is for.

  • Scan. Search, then a table of candidates. MAP plots the ones that carry coordinates, so you can see what is clustered downtown and what is out on a rural road. It costs no extra API: the coordinates come with the search you already paid for. Street tiles are fetched by the app itself from OpenStreetMap, and only once you open the map. A pin's SELECT IN TABLE jumps to its row; checks are still launched from the table.
  • Approvals. Everything generated, per artifact. ARCHIVE files a finished prospect's decided items into a collapsed section, which is a view setting and nothing more: the ledger is untouched, RESTORE brings them back, anything still waiting on you is never filed away, and new work for that prospect un-files it.
  • Settings. Your Places key, how headlines get reworded, the details printed on every artifact, and the optional brand voice, accent colour and logo.
THE FIVE LAWS

Not comments. Enforced in code.

  • 1. AI-summarised page content is never evidence. Raw source only.
  • 2. Never fabricate a review, testimonial or result. The guardrail sweep refuses rather than repairs.
  • 3. Nothing auto-sends, auto-posts or auto-prints. Approval is per item.
  • 4. Every score prints its instrument and its base.
  • 5. Discovery is the Places API, an address you type, and the business's own site, nothing else.
SETTINGS

What to set up first.

  • Google Places key. Needs Places API (New) with billing on. The only key this build reads, stored on this machine only.
  • Agent auth. How headlines get reworded. Auto resolves to the claude CLI, the only path this build implements. The model cannot change a score, count, hash or fix.
  • Your details. Name and email are printed on every artifact and are required to generate anything. Brand voice, if you set one, steers how findings are worded. The closing ask is the house pitch unless you switch it to your own words, which print exactly as typed; leave your own words blank and the scorecard closes with your signature alone.
  • Brand on documents. Optional. An accent colour and a logo for the scorecard. Severity colours stay on the house scale, so a serious finding still reads as one.
GOOD TO KNOW

Limitations.

  • There is no sender in this build. The map in the scan view fetches street tiles from OpenStreetMap through the main process, and only after you open it.
  • Page discovery is best-effort; a site that hides pages behind JavaScript may read as one page, and the score is refused rather than guessed.
  • Confirmation covers homepage, robots.txt, llms.txt and sitemap.xml, plus a paste slot for each extra page a site-wide score was read from.
  • Everything the app keeps goes to one data folder next to the install. Rendering a PDF writes one temporary file to the system temp folder and deletes it afterwards.
